    How it works
    What happens to a sentence
    
  
      
    
    1. A writer puts money on it. They pick one word for how sure they are (is, very likely, likely or disputed), and the contract locks their money behind the sentence.
      2. 
    
    2. A sealed court reads the evidence. My program runs inside an Intel chip, in a locked box that even I can't open. It fetches two web pages itself, from a short approved list.
      3. 
    
    3. Three AI judges answer. Each one sees the sentence only as quoted text. So if someone hides an order in it, the judges read the order and don't follow it. Every answer is signed.
      4. 
    
    4. The contract pays or fines. The same public rule moves the money on every round. A writer who lied loses part of the bond.

    Honest limits
    What this is not
    
  
    
    
    Not a prediction market.
    The number beside each sentence is bond-backed confidence. It comes from the word the writer picked and the money they put behind it. Nobody trades it.
    
    
    Not on the main network yet.
    Every round here ran on the NEAR test network with test money. Each case page says Testnet at the top.
    
    
    Not open to any source.
    The court only reads pages from an approved list of 10, and I control that list.
    
    
    Not a swarm or a live market.
    Both are on my roadmap. Neither is built, and nothing on this site claims them.
